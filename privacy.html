Privacy Policy - Culpa

Last updated: 16 October 2026

This privacy policy explains what data the Culpa app ("Culpa", "the app", "we", "us") collects, why, and what choices you have. It applies to the Culpa app on Android (Google Play, package com.michelzondag.culpa) and on iOS (App Store). Most of it is the same on both. Where the two differ, this policy says so.

Culpa is developed by an individual developer, Michel Zondag, trading as Culpa ("the developer"), based in the Netherlands, who is responsible for the data described here. If you have questions about this policy or your data, contact info@michelzondag.com.

1. No account needed

You do not need to create an account to play, and we never ask for your name, email address or any other details that identify you directly. Your progress, purchases and settings are stored on your device. There is no sign-in with Google Play Games, Apple Game Center or iCloud, and no cloud save: your game progress does not leave your device.

For the world leaderboard, the app quietly creates an anonymous player ID for you (see §2). It is not linked to your name, email address, Google account or Apple ID, and you can delete it yourself in the app (see §9).

We do not collect your name, email address, precise GPS location, contacts, photos, or any content you did not choose to share (such as a shared solve time, which contains only a number and, if you opt in, your country).

The world leaderboard

When you finish the daily or the weekly case, Culpa sends that one solve to the world leaderboard: the solve time, how many hints it took, a two-letter country code for your flag (the one you picked, or else one worked out from your device's language and time zone settings, never from GPS), your detective rank and your level in the game (for example "Lv 14 · Inspector"), and the anonymous player ID described in §1. Other players see your time, hints, flag, rank and level.

  • Android: you may type a detective name of at most 16 characters. A name is off by default. Without one you appear as "Detective" plus the last four characters of your anonymous ID. Please do not put anything in the name field you would not want other players to see.
  • iOS: you cannot choose your own name. Everyone appears as "Detective" plus the last four characters of their anonymous ID. Because there is no player-made text, there is no report button on iOS.

Entries are stored in Google Cloud Firestore in the EU (region eur3) and can be read by anyone playing Culpa. On Android each row on the board has a report button. A report stores the reported entry, the board it is on and the anonymous ID of the player who reported it, so an entry can be reviewed and removed. You can delete your own leaderboard data at any time in the app (see §9).

Reminders (notifications)

Culpa can remind you of the daily case or your streak. These reminders are planned by the app on your device itself. No message server is involved and no notification token leaves your device. Android and iOS ask your permission before Culpa can show them. You can switch reminders off in the app (Profile) or in your device's notification settings.

Saving a share image (iOS)

If you share a solve and choose to save the image to Photos, iOS asks whether Culpa may add photos. This permission only lets the app add that image. Culpa cannot see or read your photos.

3. Who processes this data

Culpa uses the following services. Each of them is bound by its own terms and privacy policy to protect the data it receives at least as well as this policy describes.

  • Google Firebase Analytics (Android and iOS): app usage and event data, to understand how players use the game. Google Privacy Policy
  • Google Firebase Crashlytics (Android and iOS): crash and diagnostic data, to keep the app stable.
  • Google Firebase Remote Config (Android and iOS): lets us switch a feature, such as a banner test, on or off without an app update. It uses an installation ID created by Firebase.
  • Google AdMob, including its mediation partners (Android and iOS): shows ads (rewarded video, interstitial and in some cases a banner) and uses device and advertising identifiers to select and measure ads. On iOS, Culpa only requests non-personalised ads: they are chosen from context, such as the app and your approximate location, not from your behaviour in other apps. An identifier that only applies to this app or developer is still used to limit how often you see the same ad and for aggregated ad reports. Mediation partners may include other ad networks certified by Google. Each works under its own privacy policy, listed in the consent dialog in the app.
  • Google Cloud Firestore and Firebase Authentication (Android and iOS): store the world leaderboard entries described in §2 and hand out the anonymous player ID they are filed under. No sign-in, name or email address is involved.
  • Firebase App Check (Android and iOS): checks that requests to the leaderboard come from a genuine copy of Culpa, so scores cannot be faked from outside the app. On Android this uses Google Play Integrity. On iOS it uses Apple App Attest, or Apple DeviceCheck on devices that do not support App Attest.
  • Google Play Billing (Android only): processes your in-app purchases. Culpa never sees your payment details. Google Play handles payment directly.
  • Apple App Store (iOS only): processes your in-app purchases. Culpa never sees your payment details. Apple handles payment directly.
  • Meta Platforms (Facebook, Instagram) App Events (Android only; the iOS app sends nothing to Meta): tells us which Meta ads bring players to Culpa. Only if the app may request ads, and in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland only if you allowed Meta in the consent dialog, Meta receives: that the app was installed and opened, the first time you solve a case (the case number), and a purchase (product, price, currency and the store's order number), together with your device's advertising ID and an anonymous app ID that Meta's software creates on your device. No name, email address, phone number or location is sent. Meta and the developer are joint controllers for collecting and sending this data. Meta then processes it as an independent controller under its own policy, which includes delivering and improving its ads. We switch on Meta's Limited Data Use option, which Meta applies in the US states where it is required. Meta Privacy Policy

Culpa does not use Google Play Games Services or Apple Game Center. If that ever changes, this policy, the Google Play Data safety form and the App Store privacy details are updated before that version goes out.

We do not sell your data. Data is shared with these providers only to run the app, show ads, process payments and measure advertising performance.

4. Legal basis for processing

  • In the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland and other regions with similar consent rules: personalised advertising and the related analytics and attribution happen only after you agree in the consent dialog in the app (Google User Messaging Platform), shown when you first play. If you decline or only partly agree, Culpa shows non-personalised ads and limits data collection to match the choices that dialog reports back to the app. This includes Meta on Android: the app sends nothing to Meta unless you allow Meta in that dialog. On iOS, ads stay non-personalised whatever you choose.
  • Outside those regions, where local law allows it: we rely on legitimate interest to process device identifiers and interaction data for analytics, fraud prevention and advertising, balanced against your rights. Where local law requires consent, we ask for it through the same dialog, adjusted to your region.
  • No tracking on iOS: Culpa on iOS does not track you across apps and websites owned by other companies, and therefore does not ask for permission to do so (App Tracking Transparency). Ads on iOS are always non-personalised, and the app tells Google Analytics on iOS not to store advertising identifiers and not to use your data for advertising or ad personalisation.
  • Purchase data is processed because it is needed to deliver what you bought and to meet tax and accounting obligations.
  • Leaderboard data is processed because it is needed to run the world leaderboard you take part in.

5. How long we keep data

  • Analytics data: kept by Google Analytics for at most 14 months.
  • Crash data: Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days.
  • Purchase records: kept as long as needed to honour your purchase and for the legally required accounting period, usually up to 7 years. Google Play and Apple also keep their own records of your purchases under their own policies.
  • Local app data (progress, settings, downloaded packs): stays on your device until you uninstall the app or clear its data.
  • Leaderboard entries (solve time, hint count, country, rank and level, anonymous player ID, and on Android a name only if you typed one): kept until you delete them in the app (§9), and for at most 12 months after your last activity on the leaderboard.
  • Anonymous player ID: kept until you delete it in the app (§9). After that, Google removes it from its systems within 180 days.
  • Reports filed with the report button: kept until they have been reviewed, then deleted.

6. Children

Culpa is meant for players aged 13 and over (or the higher minimum age in your country). It is not directed at children under 13. On the App Store the app is rated 13+. On Google Play it has a PEGI 7 content rating, which describes the content, not the intended age. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. Because the app is meant for players of 13 and up, we do not mark consent requests as coming from a child, so everyone gets the same consent question. If you believe a child has given us personal data, contact info@michelzondag.com and we will delete it.

7. Your rights

Under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and similar laws elsewhere, you have the right to:

  • See the data we hold about you
  • Have incorrect data corrected
  • Have your data deleted (you can delete your leaderboard data yourself in the app, see §9)
  • Object to or restrict certain processing
  • Withdraw consent at any time (this does not affect processing already carried out)
  • Receive your data in a portable format, where this applies
  • Complain to a data protection authority. In the Netherlands, where the developer is based, this is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. You can also go to the authority in your own country.

To use any of these rights, email info@michelzondag.com. We answer within one month. Because Culpa has no accounts, we may ask for details that help us find your data, such as the approximate dates you played, your device model, the last four characters of your leaderboard name, or a purchase order number. We will never ask for a password or payment details.

8. Changing your ad choices

  • In the app: where the consent dialog applies (such as the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), you can change your choice at any time under Profile › About › Ad settings, which reopens the dialog. Elsewhere this row is not shown, because there is no consent to change. Withdrawing consent for Meta there (Android) stops the app sending anything further to Meta.
  • Android: you can delete your advertising ID or opt out of ads personalisation in your device settings, usually under Settings › Privacy › Ads (the exact path differs per phone).
  • iOS: Culpa does not track you and only shows non-personalised ads, so there is no tracking setting to change for Culpa.

9. Deleting your data

In the app (Android and iOS)

Go to Profile › About › Delete leaderboard data. The app first tells you what will be deleted and asks you to confirm. Then it:

  1. deletes your times from every daily and weekly leaderboard, under the anonymous player ID this installation uses,
  2. deletes your anonymous player ID. If Google does not allow that at that moment, the app stops using the ID on this device and tells you so. The ID then no longer has any leaderboard data,
  3. clears your detective name and any scores still waiting to be sent from your device.

You see a confirmation when it is done. If the leaderboard cannot be reached, nothing is deleted and the app tells you to try again later. Your game progress, purchases and settings are not affected: they never left your device. If you play a daily or weekly case after this, you get a new anonymous ID. Reports you filed with the report button stay until they have been reviewed.

If your version of the app does not show this option yet, update the app or email us.

Other data

Data on your device is deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its storage. To ask us to delete other data, such as analytics or crash records, purchase records, or leaderboard entries the app could not find (for example from an earlier installation), email info@michelzondag.com with:

  1. the approximate dates you used the app and your device model, and
  2. if you have it: the last four characters of your leaderboard name, your Android advertising ID, or a purchase order number (Google Play order number, or the order ID in your Apple purchase history).

We confirm deletion within one month, except where the law requires us to keep purchase or accounting records for longer.

10. Security

Data sent to the services in §3 is encrypted in transit with industry-standard TLS. App Check helps keep fake requests away from the leaderboard. We do not store payment details ourselves: Google Play (Android) and Apple (iOS) handle payment directly.

11. International data transfers

Google, Apple and, on Android, Meta may process and store data outside your country, including in the United States, under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, standard contractual clauses or equivalent safeguards. See each provider's privacy policy for details.

12. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when the app changes or the law changes. The "Last updated" date at the top shows the latest version. Where required, important changes are also shown in the consent dialog in the app.
